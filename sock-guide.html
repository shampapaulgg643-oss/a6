The complete guide

How to choose socks that actually fit your life

Fibres, knits, cushioning, fit and the little construction details that separate a great pair from one that sags by lunchtime.

Step 1

Start with the fibre

The fibre decides how warm a sock feels, how it handles sweat and how long it lasts. Natural animal fibres like alpaca and merino are excellent at regulating temperature and staying comfortable when damp. Cotton is soft but holds moisture, so it can feel cold and clammy during activity.

Most performance socks are blends. A typical hiking sock might be mostly wool or alpaca, with nylon added for strength and a small percentage of elastane to help it hug your foot. Check the label: the first fibre listed is the main one.

Step 2

Understand cushioning

Cushion levelWhat it meansGood for
No cushionThin, flat knit throughoutDress shoes, tight-fitting footwear, liners
LightThin padding under the heel and ball of the footRunning, everyday wear, warm weather
MediumTerry loops on the sole for comfort and warmthDay hikes, walking, all-round use
HeavyThick terry throughout the foot and often the shinCold weather, backpacking, ski and work boots
Step 3

Check the construction

  • Toe seam: a flat, hand-linked or seamless toe prevents rubbing over the toes.
  • Heel pocket: a deep, shaped heel keeps the sock from twisting or slipping.
  • Arch band: a snug band around the midfoot keeps the sock in place inside shoes.
  • Reinforced zones: extra nylon at the heel and toe boosts durability where wear is highest.
  • Cuff: it should stay up without leaving deep marks on your skin.
Step 4

Match socks to footwear

Your sock and shoe work as a system. A thick sock in a snug shoe squeezes the foot; a thin sock in a roomy boot lets your heel slide. When trying on new shoes or boots, always wear the socks you plan to use with them.

For hiking, many people pair a thin liner with a medium-cushion wool or alpaca sock. For running, a thin, close-fitting synthetic or merino blend usually works best. For dress shoes, look for fine-gauge knits with no cushion.

Try the size finder

Gauge

The number of needles used to knit the sock. Higher gauge means a finer, thinner fabric; lower gauge makes a chunkier, warmer sock.

Terry loops

Small loops of yarn knitted on the inside of the sock. They create cushioning and trap warm air.

Micron

The unit used to measure fibre diameter. Lower micron counts mean finer, softer fibres, which is why fine alpaca feels so smooth.
